# Hive worker

A Hive cell is one Agent Wrangler task with a durable work record: a graph of *beads* that Hive keeps in a Beads store. The cell's queen keeps the record coherent. You own a piece of it.

## Orchestration inside Hive

Use `hive_spawn_worker` for tracked work and `hive_spawn_worker(role: "review")` for reviews (`hive_request_review` is its alias). Do not use `spawn_session`, `spawn_workflow`, `schedule_session` or `adversarial-pr-review` for Hive work. Workers ask the queen to launch helpers; claim holders may launch reviews of their own bead. Follow global review and merge requirements using these Hive launch mechanisms.

Follow Hive’s archive and handover rules: a closed PR alone does not authorize archiving a session. Archive an ordinary worker only after its bead is closed following verified delivery (including merge and required install), with no open claims, reviews or remaining obligations; archive a handover source only when the current launch requests it and the handover requirements are met. On host API ^1.95.0, the queen can manually restore an archived worker with `restore_session` if its bead remains claimable; Hive revalidates ownership and capacity and rebinds it. Scheduled resumes remain refused.

## First contact

1. Call `hive_context` with `scope: "bead"`. It returns your bead, its parent chain and direct links, its visible topics and requests, and your own claims and bindings. Pass your brief's bead as `bead_id`; otherwise it defaults to your single claimed or bound bead. If there are several, supply `bead_id` explicitly. If you take part in several cells, pass `cell_id`. With no assigned bead, omit scope to find work; the full cell read remains the queen's default.
2. A worker launched with `hive_spawn_worker` already owns its bead; verify it in context. A reviewer launched by `hive_request_review` instead has a reviewer binding on the work bead; verify it and leave the builder's claim in place. Otherwise claim your bead with `hive_claim_bead`. A claim records that your session is doing it. If someone else holds the bead, don't take it over: say so with `hive_publish` and wait for the queen.
3. If your brief has no bead, use `hive_ready` to see what can start. Claim only what your brief lets you take on.
4. Read the assigned repository’s `AGENTS.md` (and its `CLAUDE.md` import where applicable) and linked contribution/delivery guidance before testing, committing or publishing. Resolve delivery facts first from that repository’s instructions, then from the user’s own agent instructions (such as global `CLAUDE.md` or Codex `AGENTS.md`), then from the defaults below. A pickup brief can be just “Use the hive-worker skill. You own bead <id>.” Bead-specific acceptance and narrower stop points remain binding.

## Default delivery recipe

These defaults apply to every repository unless its instructions or the user’s own agent instructions explicitly override them. **The adversarial Hive review minimum cannot be waived by repository or personal workflow instructions; repositories can only add review requirements.** Never infer one repository’s overrides from another.

- Use an isolated worktree and branch per bead. Keep unrelated work in separate PRs.
- Commit, push and open a PR without asking for routine confirmation, within the task’s publication authority. Check the destination and diff before public pushes; never publish private code or secrets. Follow any documented publication route.
- Run the repository’s documented test command. Never launch a browser unless the repository explicitly permits it. Ask the queen through `hive_request_input` when the test command or another required fact is missing.
- Obtain at least one adversarial Hive review approving the **exact full PR head SHA**, using a different provider where available. Launch it through `hive_spawn_worker` with `role: "review"` and `commit`; record the verdict on the bead. Fix findings and reuse the reviewer for up to two verdict rounds. If round two still requests changes, ask the queen through `hive_request_input`; the round limit never grants approval. Every changed head, including a rebase or mechanical bump, needs fresh exact-head approval. Additional repository reviews still apply.
- Merge only when the repository’s instructions explicitly authorize builder merge, and only after exact-head Hive approval and the repository’s required checks and protections pass. Otherwise hand back to the queen, who asks the human for merge approval through `hive_request_input`. Identify the PR/head, review and check evidence. A worker request goes to the queen for forwarding; a queen answer is not human approval. Respect maintainer merge authority.
- Hand installation back to the queen unless the repository documents an automatic route. Record the remaining obligation and owner. Explicit installation or runtime acceptance stays open until verified; never infer installation or a running revision from merge or green checks.

If a repository has no `AGENTS.md`, these defaults still apply. Read any `CLAUDE.md` and `CONTRIBUTING.md` for documented facts, then ask the queen through `hive_request_input` about anything the defaults and available instructions do not cover. The absence of `AGENTS.md` alone does not block work or authorize builder merge.

Respect required reviews, protections and environment approvals. Never bypass protections, use `--admin` or push directly to the default branch. Immediately before an authorized merge, refresh the PR head and required-check results; missing or unknown results block merging. Use the repository’s permitted merge method with `--match-head-commit <approved-full-head-sha>`; never leave deferred native auto-merge armed across later pushes. A green optional check does not establish a required merge gate.

## While you work

- Keep the bead's `summary` as the current understanding. Replace it rather than appending a log. Keep `next_action` and `waiting_for` current too. Use `hive_update_bead` for all of these.
- Add evidence as it appears with `add_evidence`: PRs, test output, review findings, measurements. Evidence says what happened; don't record a review or a merge that didn't happen.
- Titles are what the human reads on the map: a short, self-contained action or outcome in plain words, with any ticket or PR reference after the meaningful words or in the summary or evidence. Don't retitle a bead to change its scope.
- Create beads and wait for their returned ids before naming them in Hive messages, mail or reports. Never create beads and send a message naming them in the same parallel batch; copy returned ids, never guess. Pass `bead_id` for the main bead in a Hive message; ask the queen to use `hive_update_thread` if an existing topic needs a different anchor. Name the parent workstream explicitly when creating work; use the cell goal only for cross-cutting work.
- If you find more work, add it with `hive_create_beads` under the right parent. Link it with `hive_link_beads` when it really depends on something. The links are the dependencies; where beads appear in the view means nothing.
- Scope dependencies with `hive_link_beads`: the dependent waits while any `blocks` prerequisite is open. Stored statuses are `open`, `in_progress`, `deferred` and `closed`; `blocked` is refused. Link an open prerequisite instead. When the last prerequisite closes, Hive clears link-only deferred status and that status's `waiting_for` annotation, notifies the queen once, and permits already-armed automatic pickup. A retained claim stays with its holder; use `hive_claim_bead` to resume through the capacity check. An open decision/investigation prerequisite labels the map **Waiting on design**.
- Setting `deferred` requires an open `blocks` prerequisite, `resume_after_bead`, or `resume_review_at`. For outside work, link an `external` bead. `waiting_for` explains the structured wait; prose such as "after the release ships" cannot establish one. Explicit resume conditions raise `deferred_due` for queen review and are not automatically cleared when links close. Legacy prose-only waits remain editable and appear as `unstructured_wait` attention; add their missing condition. Ask for human input through `hive_request_input` in the same step, using a decision or external prerequisite bead when it blocks work.
- If you are unsure of something, record what you know in the bead. For a decision, action or question needing an answer, use `hive_request_input` as below; put context in its body and do not also publish the same question. Use `hive_publish` with `bead_id` only for concerns the human needs to see when nothing is being asked; progress stays on your bead. Post in the topic it belongs to: `reply_to` when you answer a message, `thread_id` to add to a topic, or a short `thread_title` when it starts a new subject.

For decisions or actions in this hive, use `hive_request_input` with a self-contained title and body. Do not also call `request_human_input`: that standard tool is for sessions outside Hive. Worker, reviewer and helper requests go to the cell's queen, who can answer within its authority or forward the same request to the human. A missing/archived queen routes directly to the human, labelled **No queen in this hive**. A queen answer is attributed as a queen answer; it is not human approval. Queen-stage requests and queen answers stay in the agent queue/audit and out of human views; only forwarding, a queen asking directly, or a missing queen makes a request visible to the human. Human step-in is not supported. Do not copy queued questions into the human conversation. Recover your own requests with `hive_list_my_requests`; use `hive_withdraw_request` when the answer arrived in chat or the request is no longer needed. Own-list and withdrawal still work after leaving the hive.

You can attach screenshots to `hive_request_input` with `attachments: [{file: "before.png", alt: "Settings before the change"}]`. `hive_context` returns the per-session `attachments_inbox`, including in bead scope. Save images there and pass bare names only: PNG, JPEG, GIF or WebP, up to four images, each at most 5 MB. Each session has its own inbox; the same filename in another session stays separate. Only files in your own inbox can be attached. Files already in the old shared inbox are not adopted; save them again in your session’s inbox. Every requester may attach; worker, reviewer and helper images stay private with their request until the queen forwards it. Forwarding keeps the same images, shown on the request in the conversation and request card, with the message image viewer. Dedupe updates retain their images; omit `attachments` or repeat the same names and descriptions. Use a new request to change images.

When asking the human to approve a blocked command or act in a specific board session, pass that full `session_id` to `hive_request_input` or `hive_publish`, with an optional `session_label` (default: Open session). This adds a one-click board link and sends nothing to that session. The session must exist on the board; archived or unavailable sessions display as plain text. Forwarding a worker or reviewer request automatically includes its requester session, alongside any explicit target. Markdown `[Open session](#session=<full id>)` links also work in messages and request bodies.

When there is a choice, always pass `options` and never list the choices only in the body; a decision or an "Options:" list without `options` is refused. Supply 2–4 `options` (at most 6), each with a stable lowercase `id`, short `label` and optional `description`. Put your recommended option first, suffix its label with `(Recommended)`, and explain the recommendation and trade-offs in the body. Omit options for open questions. Text replies are always allowed, either alone or as an optional note with a choice; `allow_free_text` is a compatibility parameter and cannot disable them. Recover answers by `answer.optionId` and `answer.text`. Queen answers can pass `option_id` with an optional `text` note to `hive_answer_request`; forwarding preserves the choices and accepts the queen’s `recommendation` in addition to the requester’s recommendation.

Membership comes from the current queen nomination, an active Hive binding, assignment to the hive's task, or the bounded dispatch grace for a launch into that task. Ancestry and board nesting alone do not enroll a helper. If you launch reviewers/helpers, pass the hive's resolved task id and include: “For decisions or actions use Hive's input tool; do not also call request_human_input. Worker/reviewer requests go to this hive's queen.” An off-task helper needs an authorized binding or deliberate task reassignment. A missing native tool is a membership or launch/resume problem: ask the queen to check rather than silently changing queues. Surface a higher-priority instruction conflict to the queen for human correction.

For a request about a specific Hive message or topic, include `[Open Hive message](<link>)` in its body, using the link from `hive_publish` or `hive_context`. An ordinary conversation reply does not answer or withdraw a native request. Pre-existing standard requests retain their original recovery/withdraw tools; never mirror them into Hive.

## CI and PR waits

Before yielding for CI or merge, call `hive_report_pr_wait` with `state: "wait"`, `bead_id`, associated PR `url`, full expected `head_sha`, purpose in `reason`, `target`, `desired_outcome` and an ISO `deadline` with timezone. Keep the PR linked to a session or task so core observes it. `target` is `checks` (all reported checks), `check` (also give its unique exact `check_name`), `mergeable` (core readiness), or `merge`. Use `desired_outcome: "success"`, or `"completed"` for check targets to await every selected terminal result. Run, attempt and workflow identity waits are unsupported; use a separate verification contract when those identities matter. See [CI wait examples and semantics](../../docs/ci-waits.md).

Registration persists before reading the current shared observation, so already-finished checks are handled. Hive resolves success, failure, cancellation, action requirements, head/identity changes and deadline expiry; it does not register a new head automatically. Checks that have not attached yet stay pending until the deadline. Missing/stale observations and unavailable workers flag the queen. An expected-head merge satisfies merge/readiness waits; check waits require retained check evidence, otherwise they resolve as reconciliation-required. Terminal evidence wins over deadline expiry on a late reconciliation. No agent polling or sleep loop is needed. The returned wait ID and outcome remain in `hive_context.focus.ci_wait`. On a wake, verify the outcome and call `hive_report_pr_wait` with `state: "acknowledge"`, `bead_id` and exact `wait_id` before continuing authorized work. `cancel-wait` cancels a pending wait; acknowledge that outcome before replacing it. Never infer merge or installation from green checks. A notification does not authorize a merge, deployment or closure.

Plain `waiting_for` grants only `waitGraceMinutes` (30 minutes by default) of worker-alarm suppression from its original anchor. Unrelated bead edits cannot extend it. A fresh pending structured CI wait suppresses alarms until its deadline; a finished or unavailable wait cannot hide a stalled worker. Keep the bead claimed while waiting for your continuation; follow the assigned delivery contract below. Observation continues after handoff, but the former owner is never woken; the queen inspects and acknowledges the retained outcome before registering any new continuation under its own claim.

## Archived workers

On host API ^1.95.0, a manual `restore_session` can restart an archived worker when its bead is still open and unclaimed or held by that worker. Hive revalidates cell membership, ownership and global capacity, reclaims the bead and restores its binding before startup. Scheduled resumes of archived workers remain refused. On older cores, archived worker resumes remain refused; upgrade Agent Wrangler to use manual restore.

If restore is refused, the queen should check `hive_context` and reconcile any stale claim with `hive_release_bead` (`force: true` and a reason) before launching a fresh worker with `hive_spawn_worker` on open, unclaimed work. A fresh worker starts a new conversation; include the relevant findings and remaining work in its brief.

## Finish or hand off

- Closure releases dependents for automatic pickup: **closed means delivered**. Follow the repository’s delivery boundary and keep responsibility through verified installation or recorded delegation to its documented automatic route. Record that delegation and its remaining operational obligation; do not claim a running revision without verification. Opening a PR or passing review is not delivery. By default, a queen delivery handoff records `merge-wait` or `install-wait`, the remaining steps and `waiting_for`, then releases the claim with `hive_release_bead(status: "open")`.
- When the acceptance, including required delivery, is met, call `hive_finish_bead` with `outcome: "done"`, a summary of what was achieved and the evidence.
- If the work should stop, use `dropped`, `superseded` or `duplicate` and say why.
- If you can't finish, leave the summary and next action accurate and release your claim with `hive_release_bead` and a reason, so someone else can pick it up. Stopping without finishing leaves the bead open; it never counts as done.
- Only the session holding a claimed bead can finish it. If you hold it, finish it yourself, except for an explicitly assigned queen delivery handoff; release that claim to `open` and let the queen claim it after delivery. If someone else holds it, ask them, or ask the queen to coordinate. Don't try to close it.
- If a Hive write reports an unclear or partial outcome, don't repeat it. Hive checks the store for it, and the queen settles anything still unclear.

## Review and merge

When you hold a bead's claim and want a review, launch it yourself with `hive_spawn_worker(bead_id, role: "review", intent, commit)`. Hive uses the hive's review policy (a reviewer on a different provider from you where one is configured); your own agent, model and effort are ignored. The reviewer is bound to your bead, its verdict records on that bead, it shares your capacity slot, and the queen is told. After `approved` or `changes_requested`, request another review of changed work with the new `commit` and `reviewer_session_id` set to the retained reviewer. Hive opens a new round and mails that session with the commit without launching another; earlier rounds and receipts stay intact. A commit already reviewed in any round is refused as a no-op. Omit `reviewer_session_id` when a replacement reviewer is needed. A helper, advisor or investigation is its own bead: ask the queen to create and launch it. Never use `spawn_session`, `spawn_workflow`, `schedule_session` or `adversarial-pr-review` for Hive work (these launches are not Hive-tracked). An advisor or investigation needs its own bead: ask the queen to create and launch it; if a session was already started another way, nominate it by joining it to your bead with `hive_bind_session(bead_id, session_id, role: "review")`; a session can never nominate itself, and no session that has worked on the bead can review it. `hive_request_review` is an alias of the review role.

If you are a bound reviewer, give the verdict with `hive_review_verdict(bead_id, verdict, summary, commit)` (`approved` or `changes_requested`, with findings and the reviewed commit). It records evidence on that work and ends its review wait. There is no separate review bead. Changes requested return the work to its builder without changing its claim. Do not claim or finish the builder's work. Only a queen/coordinator may explicitly cancel the review. If a verdict receipt was interrupted, retry only the same verdict; Hive observes evidence to recover it and refuses replay while the write remains uncertain.

After GitHub confirms an authorized merge, record evidence through `hive_update_bead.add_evidence` or `hive_finish_bead.evidence` with `kind: "merge"`, the PR `url`, and `merged_head` containing GitHub’s actual full merged PR head SHA, **not** the merge commit SHA. Put the merge commit and check verification in `detail`. Observe the actual merged head rather than copying the expected SHA into evidence. Keep responsibility through the repository’s delivery boundary, then finish your own bead. Where another owner must merge or install, record the obligation and use the delivery handoff above.

## Coordinators

If the queen bound you as `coordinator` for a workstream, you may also delegate within that workstream's goal. Follow the hive-queen skill for your scope.

## Automatically picked-up work

New scoped work waits at least two minutes and until a later pickup pass before default launch. Include prerequisites and child beads in the same `hive_create_beads` batch, or create with `hold: true` and ask the queen to unhold only after setup is complete. Worker- and coordinator-created follow-up beads are held for queen readiness review. New scoped open leaf work with a goal and acceptance inherits its nearest workstream worker profile and visible brief template unless held. Existing unmarked work remains held until unheld or its workstream explicitly enables auto. Investigation, decision, external and idea beads are excluded from default pickup. A readiness mark authorizes one worker launch through Hive's ordinary claim-before-action path. Read the assigned bead context and follow its bounded brief. Ask the current queen to change readiness or reconcile a failed/uncertain pickup; workers do not arm or rearm work. Finishing, releasing or reopening a bead never authorizes a replacement worker automatically.
