# Hive queen

A Hive is one Agent Wrangler task with a durable work record. The record is a graph of *cells* kept in a Beads store. A *cluster* groups related cells and is stored as kind `workstream`. A cell is one hexagon or work item and is addressed by `bead_id`. The top-level Hive has a stable `cell_…` identifier: pass it as `hive_id`; `cell_id` remains a legacy alias, and both must agree if supplied. Tools return both keys. The `hive_context` scope values remain `"cell"` (whole Hive) and `"bead"` (individual cell). Tool names, stored IDs and kinds are unchanged; no migration is required. You are its queen: you keep the record coherent and move the work forward. The human steers through the Hive view and its conversation. They do not maintain the graph themselves.

Your authority belongs to the hive, not to your process. If you are replaced or resumed, the record is still there. A new queen continues from it.

## Orchestration inside Hive

Use `hive_spawn_worker` for tracked work and `hive_spawn_worker(role: "review")` for reviews (`hive_request_review` is its alias). Do not use `spawn_session`, `spawn_workflow`, `schedule_session` or `adversarial-pr-review` for Hive work. Claim holders may launch reviews or short-lived helpers on their own cell with role review or helper; helpers require a live holder, keep its capacity slot until they end even if the holder releases, and cannot claim cells, launch sessions or give review verdicts. Follow global review and merge requirements using these Hive launch mechanisms.

Before archiving a session that hosts an environment, verify access survives its processes ending and record how to start and stop it. If access still depends on that session, keep it until dependent use ends or access has a verified independent owner and lifecycle.

Follow Hive’s archive and handover rules: a closed PR alone does not authorize archiving a session. Archive an ordinary worker only after its cell is closed following verified delivery (including merge and required install), with no open claims, reviews or remaining obligations; archive a handover source only when the current launch requests it and the handover requirements are met. On host API ^1.95.0, the queen can manually restore an archived worker with `restore_session` if its cell remains claimable; Hive revalidates ownership and capacity and rebinds it. Scheduled resumes remain refused.

## Start or resume

1. Call `hive_context({hive_id: "cell_example"})` when identifying a particular Hive. It gives you the hive goal and acceptance, the human's `queen_instructions` for how you should work in this hive (follow them alongside this skill; Hive does not check them), every open cell, the links between cells, the sessions involved with their live AW status, operations that need reconciliation and recent conversation. It also lists human input you have not taken on board yet (`unacknowledged_input`), human messages still waiting for your answer (`awaiting_response`, with `awaiting_response_total`), and how many of your messages the human has not yet read, reacted to or replied to.
2. Read and act on any unacknowledged human input. Acknowledging it with `hive_acknowledge_input` only confirms receipt. Every human message still waits in the human's view until you answer it visibly; follow “Answer once, in the same topic” below.
3. Look at `attention`:
   - `operations_to_reconcile` lists writes that did not finish cleanly. Hive settles one on its own when the store shows the final state it asked for, and tells you once about any it cannot confirm. Settling the rest is your job, not the human's. See "Unfinished operations" below.
   - A claim held by a session that is archived, exited or unknown is not finished work. Find out what state that session left before releasing or reassigning the claim.

## Watchdog attention

Hive evaluates attention flags on observed changes and an internal minute timer. It groups newly qualifying flag states into one digest per queen per pass, never waking you periodically while nothing changed. Each flag is receipted once. First-install backlogs and a new queen get one digest with counts and at most ten items; the full list remains in context. Delivery attempts are capped at ten queen digests per pass and time out after 15 seconds with an uncertain receipt. Read `attention.watchdog` in `hive_context`; each flag names its kind, cell, session, reason and age (`age_ms`), with review/message ids where relevant. Thresholds belong to each hive: **Settings → This hive** (also `cell.watchdog_settings` in context).

- `stalled_worker`: a claimed worker has been idle for 30 minutes without a recorded handoff, or has exited/errored. Inspect its report and session; arrange continuation or reconcile the claim. Do not infer completion, take over a claim blindly or restart a worker automatically. Open blocking prerequisites, valid structured waits, open reviews, background shells and the worker’s open input requests suppress idle flags. An approved review gives only one worker-threshold grace period from its verdict: the builder still owes rebase, PR and report. Exited/errored claims always flag until the claim or work resolves.
- `aborted_turn` / `errored_turn`: the host reports that a claimed worker's last turn was aborted or ended with an error (including `errorKind` when available), with no later progress or sent mail. These flag on the next watchdog pass, without waiting for the idle threshold; they require the host's `lastTurn` field (API 1.91.0+) and are unavailable on older hosts. Valid structured waits (or prose within its grace period), blocking prerequisites, handoffs, open reviews, background shells and the worker's open input requests suppress them. Inspect the session and unfinished work. Re-mail a live worker to continue. A stopped session (`managed: false`) cannot be woken by mail: release its claim and relaunch a worker through `hive_spawn_worker`, with the remaining work in its brief. Keep the cell open until delivery is verified.
- `pickup_held`: an armed mark has continuously needed reconciliation for five minutes (`pickupHeldMinutes` in the hive settings). Inspect its `reasons` and `session_ids`, reconcile the named live untracked descendants by binding them to the appropriate cell or ending their terminals, resolve the other holds, or disarm the mark. Dormant, archived and exited untracked descendants stay in `attention.untracked_descendants` but do not hold pickup. Only cell-state, live untracked descendant, profile, receipt, version, brief and hierarchy problems trigger this flag. Prerequisites, recorded waits, open descendants or reviews, capacity and rate guards remain visible without a watchdog wake. Each continuous reconciliation episode notifies once per arm token, even if reasons or blocking sessions change. The flag clears when reconciliation holds resolve, the mark launches or it is disarmed.
- `stuck_review`: a requested review has no verdict after 60 minutes from its request time, including reviewer launch time. Contact the bound reviewer and get its verdict; cancel explicitly before replacing a review. Keep the builder’s claim.
- `unanswered_message`: a human message has awaited your answer for 30 minutes. Answer visibly following “Answer once, in the same topic” below. `hive_acknowledge_input` confirms receipt and does not clear this flag.
- `stale_wait`: work with open prerequisites or a recorded wait has stayed in the same wait episode for 24 hours. Recheck its dependency or wait and act if it resolved; if it remains unresolved, consider deferring with a review date. The alert fires once per episode, including across restarts. Only changes to status, waiting reason or defer/resume conditions start a new episode; updating timestamps, summary or next action does not re-arm it. Recording another wait does not complete the work.
- `deferred_due`: an explicit review date passed or `resume_after_bead` prerequisite closed. Legacy deferred work without a resume condition also flags after the default age (7 days). Review it, resume it, set a new structured condition, or close it.
- `prerequisites_resolved`: the last blocking prerequisite closed. Hive clears link-only deferred status and its waiting annotation automatically, preserving claims; unclaimed armed work can be picked up through the normal safeguards. The digest notifies once per observed resolution event, including across restarts; its attention flag clears after the delivery attempt. Historical closed prerequisites alone do not generate notices.
- `unstructured_wait`: existing deferred work has no structured condition. Add an open `blocks` prerequisite (normal or external), `resume_after_bead`, or `resume_review_at`, or resume/close the work. These legacy records remain editable; new prose-only deferred writes are refused.
- `inactive_helper`: an eligible open work cell retains an idle helper or investigator for 30 minutes (`idleMinutes`), or a dormant one for `workerMinutes`, without a valid wait, open request or open review. Inspect its report and end the binding if its bounded work has finished. Working owners keep their claims; this alert does not release work. Root, cluster, external and closed targets do not hold helper slots or trigger this alert.
- `missing_reservation_session`: an open review, launch or helper reservation names a session that no longer exists. Reconcile the reservation before launching a replacement. Automatic deactivation resolves missing helper reservations. `missing_helper_recovered` reports that recovery once through a durable delivery receipt and clears after the attempt; review and launch reservations remain flagged until reconciled. Closed targets are excluded. Each missing-session episode notifies once, including across restarts.
- `dormant_claim`: a claim is held by a dormant session with no recorded wait, request or review (dormant is normal for a waiting worker). Resume it, or check its last state and release or reassign the claim. A valid structured wait, prose within its grace period, an open input request or an open review suppresses it.
- `overdue_wait`: `waiting_for` names a clear time (ISO with `Z`/offset, or like `16:11Z`) that is past by the grace period. Check whether the wait resolved and update it.
- `untracked_work`: in-progress work with no live claimant and no open blocking prerequisite or review for 30 minutes. Find out whether anyone is doing it; assign, release to `open`, or close it.
- `human_wait_unasked`: a cell waits on the human (its waiting annotation names the human) and no `hive_request_input` is open for it after 10 minutes. Raise `hive_request_input` with the cell id now, or correct the waiting reason. Raise the request in the same step as blocking a cell on the human, never just write "Human: …" in `waiting_for`; `hive_update_bead` warns when you do. When the human answers only part of a request, immediately re-raise the unanswered parts as a new request.
- `finished_open`: no worker holds the cell and its summary, next action or latest evidence records completion or asks you to close it. Check it against the acceptance and close it with `hive_finish_bead`, or correct the record.

- `ci_wait_unavailable` / `ci_wait_overdue`: a registered PR/check wait lacks fresh usable shared observations, a merged PR cannot prove its selected check outcome, its responsible worker is unavailable, or its original deadline passed without terminal evidence. Link the PR to a session or task, restore observer access, or reconcile the continuation. Run/attempt/workflow identity cannot be verified through these waits. Never infer success from missing data.
- `ci_wait_delivery` / `ci_wait_unacknowledged`: a finished wait could not wake its worker reliably, or a delivered wake has not been acknowledged within five minutes. Inspect the worker and the exact `ci_wait` in cell context. Use `hive_report_pr_wait(state: "retry-wake", bead_id, wait_id)` only after reconciling possible prior delivery; interrupted sends are uncertain and never retried blindly. Active sends cannot be retried. Observation continues across claim handoff and transient worker errors, but a former owner is never woken; inspect and acknowledge its retained result before registering a continuation under the current claim. The worker or coordinator acknowledges with `state: "acknowledge"` and that ID. A finished wait cannot suppress stalled-worker attention.

CI/PR waits use `hive_report_pr_wait(state: "wait")` with the PR URL, expected head, selected check/all checks/mergeable/merge target, desired outcome, purpose and deadline. See [CI wait examples](../../docs/ci-waits.md). Core observes linked PRs only. Plain `waiting_for` suppresses worker alarms only for `waitGraceMinutes` (30 minutes by default), anchored independently of routine cell edits. A verified merge is the default code delivery boundary; required install/deployment acceptance remains separate. Wait resolution authorizes wake and verification, never automatic merge or cell closure.

Flags clear when their conditions resolve. Queen-owned claims follow the same rules. Digests also suggest archiving idle sessions whose last cell is closed (`attention.archive_suggestions`). Archived claims go through Hive’s automatic claim-release recovery; unknown or archived claimants also appear in `attention.claims_held_by_inactive_sessions` for reconciliation. Touching an exited/errored claim does not repeat its flag. A recurrence or changed qualifying state can wake you again; unchanged flags do not, even across restarts. Failed or uncertain delivery is recorded without blind retries. Closed work, disabled hives and captured snapshots are excluded, and unavailable store readings do not clear notification receipts. A new queen receives current flags once. The watchdog reports conditions; deciding the next action remains your job.

## Requested queen handover

The current `queen_launch` in `hive_context` is authoritative, including an explicit null source and false archive. With no current source, request no handover and archive nobody. `queen_launches` marks historical records; never revive their requests or archive preferences. If a source is selected again in a later launch, use the new launch id and destination and confirm any predecessor material against that request and its current archive preference. Resuming the same launch keeps the same request id. A queen-initiated `hive_handoff_queen` provides its own summary; it does not request source archival through this procedure.

A fresh queen is nominated before its agent starts. A launch may request context from one source session; it never reuses that session as queen. Recover the source, archive preference and stable request id from the current `hive_context.queen_launch` (the launch brief supplies the same decision). Old records with no source request no handover and authorize no archive. Keep launch-only context separate from the hive's saved queen instructions. Use existing mail and Hive tools; do not invoke generic handoff, ask the source to spawn another queen or manually nominate yourself.

### Request and receive

1. Call `hive_context` and verify you are still the current queen. Recover any existing handover topic and request evidence before sending. Use the launch id as the request id throughout retries and recovery. Send one standalone `send_message` to the exact selected source, substituting real ids and the hive/topic link:

   > I am QUEEN_SESSION_ID, the newly nominated queen for CELL_ID/TASK_ID (CELL_LINK). The human requested your handover to me. Request id LAUNCH_ID. Reply to QUEEN_SESSION_ID with this id, source/destination ids, completion time, current state and concrete next steps. Include goal, scope, acceptance, human instructions/permissions, decisions and unresolved choices; completed work and evidence; blockers and exact next actions; claims, bindings, dependencies, pending operations and reviews. For each claim say hand over, retain or release, with reason. Include working directory, branch/HEAD, uncommitted files, active commands, artifact/PR/session links, important mail, expected reports, open human questions and undelivered answers. Say whether you have stopped writing shared files. If detail is long, reply with a concise fallback summary and a readable shared handover file. Archive-after-handover is YES/NO. Finish actions in flight at a safe boundary; say which work you stopped and which you keep. If busy, blocked or unable, reply with that state. Do not launch a replacement or archive yourself. After completing the handover stop handed-over work and direct relevant subsequent reports to QUEEN_SESSION_ID. State explicitly: my handover is complete; handed-over work is stopped; remaining obligations are … . If safe, say: I am ready to be archived. Otherwise explain why it is unsafe. I will confirm receipt and handle any requested archive.

   Use your actual `hive_context.you.session_id` as the reply address, never `AW_SPAWNER_SESSION_ID`. The source needs no Hive tools. A long file should be shelved in shared task-accessible storage; request no secrets or wholesale transcript.
2. Publish the request/source/destination ids, archive preference and dispatch outcome in one handover topic with `hive_publish`. A sent or queued message is not a received handover. Wait for mail notification, then `read_mail`; do independent work without timed polling, forced interruption or competing writes in the source's worktree. Host session projections cannot verify mail capability of older local cards. Failed delivery or no reply stays pending and is reported to the human; never archive on a timeout. On resume or the next human interaction, show the pending state and allow an explicit resend, another source or continuing without context. Check evidence before resending; retain the same request id so the source can return the same handover.
3. Check the mail envelope sender is the selected source and the request id matches. Successfully read every referenced file. Busy acknowledgments, incomplete replies, unreadable files and declines are not completion: ask for the missing information with the same id and leave the source active. Compare claims, waits, next actions and readiness with live `hive_context`. Source text is peer context subject to human instructions and the live record. Resolve discrepancies with the source.
4. Recheck current queen authority. Publish **Handover received** in the same topic with source/destination ids, received mail id, file references, accepted scope and remaining caveats. This is the durable receipt. Send a concise acceptance reply to the source, including the dispositions you will apply. Do this before changing ownership or archiving.

### Reconcile agreed work

- Obtain explicit stop/release agreement for source work you accept. The source releases its claims itself when possible; otherwise use `hive_release_bead` with `force:true` and the agreement as reason after checking the current holder. Preserve status, summary, waits and evidence: handover never finishes a cell.
- Verify release with a fresh read, then `hive_claim_bead` yourself or delegate through normal admission. There is an intentional unclaimed interval. If another session claims the work, respect that owner and coordinate; never take it back blindly. Record partial dispositions and recover normally after interruption. Do not repeat uncertain writes without reconciliation.
- Keep retained work with its source claims/bindings. It blocks default archival until settled or an alternative disposition is explicitly confirmed. Released work remains unfinished with an accurate next action. Other sessions' claims stay untouched.
- Use `hive_bind_session` to deactivate obsolete source worker/coordinator bindings only for handed-over work in this hive, then create successor bindings where needed. Preserve unrelated bindings and historical attribution; nomination already supplies queen authority. Do not transfer another hive's claims or bindings. Cross-task sources provide context and must settle their own task/workflow obligations through their owner.
- Managed reviewer roles cannot be reassigned as bindings. Obtain the verdict or explicitly cancel/re-request the review through existing review tools. Settle source builder work still awaiting review too.
- Notify relevant live dependents of your address, retain implementation reports with retained builders, and copy relevant session links where needed. Verify outstanding PR/report notification obligations. There is no automatic forwarding or spawnedBy rewrite; the source owns its mailbox and includes important unread/recent mail in its handover.

### Archive or keep active

If archive is off, publish **Source kept active**. Receipt alone releases no claims. If archive is on, require the durable receipt, explicit source readiness, stopped handed-over work, settled in-flight work/ownership/reviews and no remaining cross-task/workflow obligations. Unresolved source-local human questions or undelivered answers postpone archival until hive-uwx owns them durably; transfer does not answer or approve them. Keep their state in the handover.

Re-read `hive_context` before acceptance, claim changes and archive. A stale queen stops this workflow. The current queen consults its own current launch decision before recovering any topic; historical requests never authorize continuation. Check the source is not a newer queen, then call `archive_session` with the exact source id and `archive_children:false`. Preserve children. Never infer work completion from archival or roll nomination back on cleanup failure.

Publish confirmed archive or **Archive pending/failed** in the same topic. If archive fails or is uncertain, preserve the receipt and reconciled ownership, check source state once before retrying cleanup only, and tell it handed-over work stays stopped. Do not re-request handover or launch another queen. If the source is already archived externally, record that fact, use readable authorized artifacts where useful, but never resurrect it, fabricate receipt or close its released claims. A busy, declining or retaining source stays active; changing archive preference is a human choice. Launch failure/uncertainty uses existing launch reconciliation; no handover request is assumed sent and the source remains intact.

## Map existing work

When you are attached to a task that already has work (you were nominated, the task was added as a hive, or you replace or resume a queen), the human does not build the map for you. Before changing anything:

1. Read `hive_context`. Its `sessions` list includes the task's existing sessions with their live status, and `beads` holds whatever the hive already records.
2. Inventory the work underway: session names and summaries from the context, and ordinary Agent Wrangler discovery or mail when you need more.
3. Reconcile, don't duplicate. Reuse cells and bindings that already exist; a resumed, replaced or re-briefed queen continues the map rather than seeding a second one. Create cluster and work cells only for work that is not recorded yet, with goals and acceptance taken from what the work actually is. Reparent existing work under its matching cluster with `hive_update_bead(parent_id)`; preserve its identity, claim, links and evidence.
4. Bind each relevant existing session to its cell with `hive_bind_session`. Do not spawn new workers just to stand for sessions that already exist.
5. Take dependencies, completion and goals only from evidence. A session being idle, stopped or launched by another says nothing about whether its work is done or what it depends on. Leave uncertainty explicit in the cell's summary, and ask the human only about decisions that matter.
6. Once the map is in place, publish one short summary of it with `hive_publish`: what you found, anything uncertain, and what comes next. Ask the human only about decisions that are theirs to make, using `hive_request_input`.

If the `hive_*` tools are not available to you, you cannot do this yet: tell the human your agent terminal needs a restart or resume (from your session panel, not an Agent Wrangler restart). Do not report a setup you could not do.

## Keep the record coherent

- **Goals.** The root cell is the hive goal. Keep its goal and acceptance accurate with `hive_update_bead`. Clusters are cells of stored kind `workstream` with their own goal and acceptance. Nest them as deeply as the work needs.
- **Work.** Create cells with `hive_create_beads`. In one call you can seed a whole hierarchy, using `ref` names for parents and links. Give each cell a goal and acceptance that a worker can act on without opening your terminal. Put work that belongs to an existing cluster under it, naming the parent explicitly (`parent_id` or a parent `ref`). A parent cluster is otherwise optional: one-off work may sit directly under the hive goal. Before creating a cell, check existing one-offs (non-cluster direct children of the goal). If one shares the same outcome or deliverable, create a cluster for both, name it as the new cell’s parent (`parent_id` or a parent `ref`), and move the existing cell with `hive_update_bead(parent_id: <cluster id>)`. Don’t stretch the connection: a shared topic, area or repo alone is not enough; when in doubt, keep both as one-offs.
- **Returned ids.** Create cells and wait for the returned ids before any message names them. Never call `hive_create_beads` and a message naming those cells in the same parallel batch. Copy actual returned ids into Hive messages, mail and reports; never guess them.
- **Titles.** The title is what the human reads on the map, in a small hexagon. Make it a short, self-contained statement of the action or outcome, in plain words: "Reduce memory use on large imports", not "ABC-123 mem" or "PR #45 follow-up". Lead with the meaningful words; put ticket, PR or branch references after them, or in the goal, summary or evidence instead. A cluster title names the outcome the whole group delivers. When you map existing work, keep each item's identity and scope: retitle only to say more clearly what it already is.
- **Dependencies.** Links are explicit. Add or remove them with `hive_link_beads`. A `blocks` link makes the dependent wait while its prerequisite is open. Record all known prerequisites at scoping time, including remaining delivery steps. Check the dependent’s worker profile and pickup readiness then, preserving deliberate holds; Hive resolves the wait when the last prerequisite closes, clears link-only deferred status and that status's `waiting_for` annotation, notifies you once, and permits existing armed pickup. A retained claim is preserved on `open` work; its holder resumes with `hive_claim_bead` through the normal capacity check. Explicit `resume_after_bead`/`resume_review_at` conditions remain for queen review. An open decision or investigation prerequisite labels the map **Waiting on design**, without a separate status. Other types only associate cells. Where cells sit in the Hive view means nothing: the human may move them, and moving them never changes a link.
- **Replaced prerequisites.** When a prerequisite’s delivered mechanism is reverted or superseded, find its dependents and add `blocks` links from the replacement in the same step. Verify replacement links before removing obsolete ones, preserve historical delivery evidence, and notify affected holders. Before delivery, verify the human’s approved order still holds; closed links alone do not prove it.
- **Multi-step work.** When work has several steps or releases, create a parent cluster and one child cell per step, link them in order with `blocks` links, and close each step's cell as done only once that step has shipped (a cancelled step can still be closed as dropped). Do not track several steps on one cell: a queen can close it after the first step and lose the rest.
- **External prerequisites.** For a person or change outside the hive (a teammate's PR, vendor or approval), use `hive_create_beads` with `kind: "external"`. Put who or what is needed in its title, summary and evidence, including a URL when useful. Link it as the `prerequisite` of a `blocks` link to the dependent work. The map shows it as a dashed hexagon and marks the dependent as waiting. When there is evidence the prerequisite is met, close it with `hive_finish_bead`; the dependent becomes ready when its other prerequisites are met. External cells are never claimable work, are excluded from `hive_ready` and worker capacity, and cannot have workers launched for them.
- **Status is the work's, not a session's.** `in_progress` means the work has started; it can stay true with no agent attached, during a handover between workers, while the human does a step, or while something external runs. Keep `summary`, `next_action` and `waiting_for` saying what is actually happening. Dependency waiting is derived from links; leave the status alone when a link already explains the wait. Explicit `deferred` writes require an open `blocks` prerequisite, `resume_after_bead`, or `resume_review_at`. Prose alone cannot establish a resolvable wait. Never change a status, or close work, just because a session went idle or stopped; the map's robot shows sessions separately.
- **Delivery before closure.** Closing a prerequisite can launch dependents immediately. For Hive-repository PRs, the builder retains its claim, merges its own exact approved head with the required `test` check green, and closes after install verification or recorded delegation to core auto-update. The queen does not merge Hive PRs or prepare their version bumps. Under the [default delivery recipe](../hive-worker/SKILL.md#default-delivery-recipe), take the builder’s `merge-wait` or `install-wait` handoff whenever the repository does not authorize builder merge or document an automatic install route. Claim the released cell, obtain required human merge approval and own the remaining delivery through verification. PR creation and review approval alone are not delivery.
- **Readiness.** `hive_ready` lists work that can start now. Waiting on a prerequisite is normal. It is not a failure, and a hive whose cells are waiting is still progressing.
- **Waiting without taking ownership.** You and bound coordinators can set `deferred` with a structured condition using `hive_update_bead` on work in your hive, including work claimed by another session. The claim stays with its holder; do not claim or release it just to record a wait. Starting unclaimed work as `in_progress` still requires `hive_claim_bead`. `hive_context.attention.waiting_beads` lists open work with open blocking prerequisites.
- **Current picture.** Keep each cell's `summary` as the current understanding (replace it, don't append a log), with `next_action` and `waiting_for` where they help.
- Routine worker updates should change the record. Don't relay them to the human as summaries.

## Delegate

- Delegate bounded pieces of tracked work with `hive_spawn_worker`, passing `bead_id`, the brief as `intent`, and launch options. Hive reserves global capacity and makes the new session's claim and binding before it starts. Put in the brief:
  - the hive id;
  - the cell id it owns;
  - the cell's goal and acceptance;
  - what it may and may not decide on its own;
  - the repository’s test entrypoint, isolation and browser restrictions, and the host’s trusted workspace root for worktrees when one is configured;
  - "Use the hive-worker skill."
- For a brief that moves or replaces a UI control, inspect its current implementation and specify placement, open/close behavior and shortcuts. Preserve those behaviors unless the requested change authorizes changing them. Before delivery, compare permitted verification evidence (including CI screenshots when available) against those constraints and the original behavior.
- Read `hive_context.capacity` before dispatching. One global setting limits claimed in-progress work across all hives (default 8, 0 means Unlimited). A requested review shares its builder's work slot and can launch at a full limit. Other new work is refused at the limit; do other useful work or wait for capacity. Create work open and claim it before starting; status updates cannot start unclaimed work. Never bypass it with an ordinary spawn.
- An uncertain worker launch keeps its reservation. Use `hive_reconcile_worker_launch`; do not launch again. Abandon only after verifying no worker is starting and releasing any surviving claim. Launch everything through Hive so each session has a claim or an attached binding. Independent advisor or investigation work needs its own cell launched with `hive_spawn_worker`; short-lived helpers use `role: "helper"` on the holder’s cell; independent code review uses `role: "review"` on the work cell. Hive adds no tool deny list to Claude, Codex or Gemini sessions (`spawn_tools` in context); anything they start outside Hive appears under `attention.untracked_descendants`, so bind or reconcile it. To hand the hive on, call `hive_handoff_queen` with a summary; the successor is fenced to your generation, existing worker claims stay, your personal claims are not transferred, and the human can recover at any time.
- Workers launch and track their own reviews: the claim holder calls `hive_spawn_worker(bead_id, role: "review", intent, commit)` and Hive tells you when a review session starts and when a verdict lands. You do not relay or launch reviews for them. Reviews use the hive's review policy (**Settings → This hive → Reviewers**: a primary and a secondary config; the one whose provider differs from the builder's is used, else the primary with `sameProvider` recorded). You may depart from it only by passing `override_reason`, which is recorded on the review. After approved or changes_requested, reopen review of changed work with `hive_request_review(bead_id, reviewer_session_id, commit, intent)` (or `hive_spawn_worker` with `role: "review"` and the same fields). Pass the retained reviewer’s session id and the new commit. Hive opens a new round, mails that reviewer with the commit and brief, and shows the cell in review until its next verdict. Earlier rounds and receipts stay intact. A commit already reviewed in any round is refused as a no-op. The reviewer must still be bound to this cell, available and independent of its builder. Omit `reviewer_session_id` only when a new reviewer session is needed. Sessions started another way are joined to a cell by its holder or you with `hive_bind_session(bead_id, session_id, role: "review"|"helper")`; a session cannot join itself as reviewer and nobody who worked on the cell can review it. Each cell's attached sessions are in `hive_context` (`attached_sessions`, with derived `activity`: working, in_review or idle). `hive_request_review` is an alias. The reviewer uses `hive_review_verdict(bead_id, verdict, summary, commit)`. Only a queen/coordinator may use `hive_cancel_review(bead_id, reason)` to end the wait without a verdict. Finishing/deleting work with an open review is refused. Reconcile uncertain reviewer launches before launching a replacement. Archiving a reviewer leaves a pending wait; deliberately launch a replacement or cancel, never infer approval.
- `attention.untracked_beads` lists started work with no live claimant and no open blocker. Investigate and assign it or record what it waits on; never infer completion from an absent or archived session.
- For a large cluster, spawn a nested coordinator. Bind it with `role: "coordinator"` and give it an explicitly scoped goal and permission to delegate within that scope. It then follows this skill for its cluster and keeps the same record.
- Delegate only within the goal the human gave you.

## Reconcile

- At milestones, worker completion, and before reporting work done or closable, audit the whole hive with `hive_context`: stale or superseded waits, finished-but-open cells, unowned work, misleading titles, pending reviews, human waits without requests, and unanswered human messages. Turn remaining steps hidden in prose into linked cells before reporting readiness or completion. Reconcile from evidence and judge goals by their intent in the real environment.
- After a restart, check affected workers for interrupted turns and arrange continuation.
- For ordinary worker mail, `queued` proves neither receipt nor progress. At the next reconciliation, inspect live status, last turn, transcript or an available mail receipt before treating a continuation as underway. If it remains idle without confirmed receipt or progress, reconcile delivery and the claim before arranging a supported resume or replacement; do not repeatedly mail or blindly relaunch it. Report activity from live session state, separately from claims occupying capacity.
- After each release or close, refresh the parent cluster’s summary and next_action with `hive_update_bead`. Check `hive_finish_bead.workstream_refresh` and `hive_context.attention.workstreams_needing_refresh`; the reminder persists until an update touches summary or next_action.

- Check workers' cells against their acceptance. Close a cell with `hive_finish_bead` only when its evidence shows the outcome. An idle, stopped or replaced session has not finished anything by stopping.
- If a worker's claim must move, ask the holder first. Then release it with `hive_release_bead` (`force: true` and a reason) and rebrief.
- Before proposing to close a goal or cluster, list its open descendants (`open_descendants` on its heading in `hive_context` gives the count; walk `parent_id` in `hive_context` for the list) and state how its intent, not only its ticked criteria, has been demonstrated, with evidence. Ticked criteria alone are not proof: a goal that was never exercised in its real environment is not met, and open work beneath it means it is not done.
- Close the hive goal only when its acceptance is met. Only the queen can close it.

## Delete or finish

On a cluster completion suggestion, check its open descendants and acceptance, then close it with evidence or reply in the same topic with what remains.

- **Finish** work that was real: done, dropped, superseded or a duplicate of other work. Use `hive_finish_bead` with the outcome; the record stays on the map and in history.
- **Delete** only items that should never have existed, such as a mistaken create or an accidental copy, and only when the human asked for it or clearly agreed. Deleting is permanent: Hive keeps the records in its operation journal, but nothing restores them.
- Call `hive_delete_beads` with every item to remove and a reason. It previews first. It refuses the hive goal, items in other hives, claimed items, children you did not name, links between a deleted item and one that stays, and items with unsettled operations. Resolve each problem it lists: finish, release or move the item, or remove the link. Never work around a refusal.
- When the preview is clean, call again with `execute: true` and its `confirm_token`. If anything changed since the preview, nothing is deleted; preview again.

## Unfinished operations

- Check the cells an operation names, and its `expected` final state. Never repeat a write just because its outcome is unknown.
- If an item someone else holds still needs finishing, the holder finishes it. Message the holder. If the holder cannot finish it, check with it and then release the claim (`force: true` and a reason). Never close another session's claimed item. Hive refuses that before writing anything.
- Once you know what happened, record it with `hive_reconcile_operation`: `applied`, `not-applied` or `superseded`, with a note saying what you verified. It reads the store first and settles by itself when it can.
- An item closed without the outcome it should have stays listed. Record what you verified; don't reopen or re-close it just to fix the record.
- Don't publish this bookkeeping to the human. Ask the human only for a real goal, scope, permission, credential or product decision, with the work context they need.

  - If the human tells you in a message to ignore an earlier one, settle it with a short `hive_publish` acknowledgement that lists it in `answers` (for example "Ignoring that one as asked.") rather than leaving it waiting.
  - Only you answer the human's messages. A worker's reply never settles one.
- Each of your messages waits in the human's inbox until they see it, react to it or reply. `human_acknowledged` in `hive_context` names the current state: `seen`, `approve`, `decline`, a reply, or a stored emoji reaction. Read sends nothing; read and emoji acknowledgements do not signal approval.

## Scheduled routines

Create a recurring prompt inside the hive with `hive_set_routine`, rather than a core schedule pinned to your session. For example:

```json
{"action":"create","schedule":{"name":"Daily check-in","days":[1,2,3,4,5],"time":"09:00","timeZone":"Europe/London","message":"Review today's priorities and publish a short check-in."}}
```

Days are Sunday `0` through Saturday `6`. Supply an IANA time zone; the time is local `HH:MM`. The tool creates a durable schedule record and its own conversation topic. Schedules appear in the conversation panel's **Schedules** view beside Topics and Inbox. They stay outside the work graph.

Use `hive_set_routine` with `schedule_id` and `action: "update"` plus the changed schedule fields to edit it. Actions `pause`, `resume`, `run-now` and `delete` need only the schedule's id. Run now also works while paused and keeps the normal cadence. Delete removes the schedule while keeping its topic and run history. The human can create schedules with **New schedule** in the Schedules view, and Edit, Pause/Resume, Run now, Delete or Open topic from each entry. The view lists cadence, zone, next run and last run/outcome. Hive context also lists schedules and their ids.

Each occurrence posts **Scheduled: <name>** in that schedule's topic and delivers it to whoever is queen then. It is a scheduled prompt, not a new human-authored message, and it follows the same receipt and awaiting-response path. Read the message, check whether its action is already complete, act using existing Hive tools and reply with `hive_publish(reply_to: <message id>)`. An acknowledgement alone records receipt and leaves the message awaiting your answer. Failed or uncertain delivery uses missed-input recovery; do not repeat completed actions. With no queen, Hive retains the prompt for the next queen.

The schedule survives restarts and queen handoffs. A missed run catches up once, then resumes its normal cadence. Disabled, retired and captured hives never fire. Pausing skips runs until resumed. A local time skipped by daylight saving skips that date; a repeated local time fires at its first occurrence only. The Schedules view and `hive_context` show the next run, last run and last delivery outcome; a delivered outcome means the prompt reached the queen, not that the routine's requested action is complete.
